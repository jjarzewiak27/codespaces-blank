Stay Safe Online, One Click at a Time

This site is here to help you recognize phishing emails, phone scams, and other tricks criminals use to steal money and personal information.

Scammers try to trick you into clicking links, opening attachments, or giving them your information. We will show you what their tricks look like and how to say “no.”

Example 1: An email that says “urgent payment needed” with spelling mistakes.

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trusted person or company (like your bank or Social Security) to trick you into giving away personal information, clicking a bad link, or sending money.

  • Emails that say “Your account will be closed in 24 hours.”
  • Texts with links that say “Package delivery problem – click here.”
  • Phone calls claiming to be the IRS or tech support asking for payment.

Real companies do not ask you for your password, full Social Security number, or credit card by email or text.

Can You Spot the Scam?

Read the short message below. Then click the button to reveal the red flags.

From: secure-banking@myb4nk-support.com

Subject: URGENT: Account Locked!!!

Dear Customer, your account has been locked for unusual activity. Please click here immediately and re-enter your password and Social Security number to restore full access. Failure to act will result in permanent account closure.

  • Strange email address: “myb4nk-support.com” is not your real bank.
  • Lots of urgency: “URGENT!!!” and “permanent account closure.”
  • Asking for Social Security number and password – real banks don’t do this.
  • Suspicious link: we don’t know where “click here” really goes.

Need Help with a Suspicious Message?

If you ever get a message that worries you, do not click on any links. Instead:

  1. Call the company using a phone number you already trust.
  2. Ask a family member or friend to look at the message with you.
  3. Use our “Check Your Cyber Smarts” page to learn more.

Quick Tips

  • If it feels rushed or scary, pause and double-check.
  • Never give personal info over email or text.
  • Type web addresses yourself instead of clicking links.
  • Use strong passwords and turn on 2-step verification when possible.
